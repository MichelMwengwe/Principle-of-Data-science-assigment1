# Student Performance Findings

## V1 - Gender differences in math vs reading

The boxplots show that female students score a little higher than male students, especially in reading. Females have an average math score of 63.6 and reading score of 72.6, while males have an average math score of 68.7 and reading score of 65.5. The difference between males and females is larger in reading than in math. However, the scores overlap, which means that not every female student scores higher than every male student. Overall, the data shows a small difference in scores between male and female students.

## V2 - Test prep impact on math

The boxplot shows that students who completed the test preparation course have higher math scores on average. Students who completed the course have an average math score of 69.7, while students who did not complete it have an average score of 64.1. Overall, students who completed the test preparation course performed better in math in this dataset. However, this does not mean that the test preparation course directly caused the higher scores.

## V3 - Lunch type and average performance

The bar chart shows that students with a standard lunch have higher average scores in math, reading, and writing. Students with a standard lunch have average scores of 70.0 in math, 71.7 in reading, and 70.8 in writing. Students with a free/reduced lunch have average scores of 58.9 in math, 64.7 in reading, and 63.0 in writing. Overall, students with a standard lunch score higher on average in all three subjects.

## V4 - Subject correlations

The heatmap shows a strong connection between math, reading, and writing scores. The correlation between math and reading is 0.82, math and writing is 0.80, and reading and writing is 0.95. Overall, students who score higher in one subject also tend to score higher in the other subjects.

## V5 - Math vs reading with trend lines by test prep

The scatter plot shows a strong connection between reading and math scores for both groups. Students who completed the test preparation course have a correlation of 0.79, while students who did not complete it have a correlation of 0.82. Overall, students with higher reading scores also tend to have higher math scores. Students who completed the test preparation course also tend to have higher scores in both subjects.
